SECURE EXAM BROWSER - EVIDENCE AND GAP MAP
Date: 2026-04-18
Prepared from: OpenAlex query results plus secure exam browser and online proctoring literature

PURPOSE
This file lists evidence from high-quality research and the major gaps found in older secure exam browser and proctoring systems.

EVIDENCE 1
Source: A systematic review of research on cheating in online exams from 2010 to 2021 (Education and Information Technologies, 2022, DOI: 10.1007/s10639-022-10927-7)
Evidence: Cheating methods evolve faster than single-control exam systems; no single control prevents all misconduct.
Gap in older SEB systems: Over-reliance on kiosk lock and browser restriction without continuous multimodal identity assurance.

EVIDENCE 2
Source: Online exam proctoring technologies: Educational innovation or deterioration? (British Journal of Educational Technology, 2022, DOI: 10.1111/bjet.13182)
Evidence: Proctoring raises trust, fairness, and student stress concerns when systems are opaque.
Gap in older SEB systems: Poor explainability and weak appeal workflow for flagged incidents.

EVIDENCE 3
Source: How Common is Cheating in Online Exams and did it Increase During the COVID-19 Pandemic? A Systematic Review (Journal of Academic Ethics, 2023, DOI: 10.1007/s10805-023-09485-5)
Evidence: Misconduct risk is context-dependent and influenced by assessment design and monitoring quality.
Gap in older SEB systems: Static policy profiles and weak risk adaptation by exam type, subject, and stakes.

EVIDENCE 4
Source: The Accuracy of AI-Based Automatic Proctoring in Online Exams (Electronic Journal of e-Learning, 2022)
Evidence: Automatic proctoring can generate false positives in realistic environments.
Gap in older SEB systems: Hard thresholds and event spikes without temporal smoothing, confidence fusion, or human-in-the-loop review.

EVIDENCE 5
Source: Remote proctoring: Lessons learned from the COVID period (Journal of Computer Assisted Learning, 2022)
Evidence: Operational failures (permissions, bandwidth, unstable devices) reduce reliability.
Gap in older SEB systems: Limited resilience to network drops, permission revokes, and low-end hardware.

EVIDENCE 6
Source: An automated online proctoring system using attention methods (Multimedia Tools and Applications, 2023)
Evidence: Multimodal signals improve detection quality when fused correctly.
Gap in older SEB systems: Camera-only or independent rule checks with no calibrated multimodal risk score.

EVIDENCE 7
Source: Proctor net: An AI framework for suspicious activity (Measurement, 2022)
Evidence: Suspicious behavior detection benefits from sequence-level modeling, not only frame-level triggers.
Gap in older SEB systems: Lack of sequential behavior modeling over time.

EVIDENCE 8
Source: A Proposed Model for Improving the Reliability of Online Proctoring (IEEE Access, 2023)
Evidence: Reliability improves with layered controls and robust incident pipelines.
Gap in older SEB systems: Weak incident evidence schema and inconsistent severity handling.

EVIDENCE 9
Source: ChatGPT: The End of Online Exam Integrity? (Education Sciences, 2024, DOI: 10.3390/educsci14060656)
Evidence: LLMs can solve many assessment tasks, especially under weak process controls.
Gap in older SEB systems: Focus on browser misuse while under-addressing AI-assisted answer generation patterns.

EVIDENCE 10
Source: ChatGPT/Copilot/Gemini impacts on assessment integrity (Australasian Journal of Engineering Education, 2024, DOI: 10.1080/22054952.2024.2372154)
Evidence: GenAI changes integrity threat models across domains.
Gap in older SEB systems: Missing policy controls and detection heuristics for AI-era cheating behavior.

EVIDENCE 11
Source: Frequency trends from retrieved abstracts (user query output)
Evidence: Most frequent reported gaps include spoofing, false positives, bias, privacy, legal/compliance, and network robustness.
Gap in older SEB systems: Anti-spoofing and fairness are often weak; privacy-by-design is not default.

EVIDENCE 12
Source: Current project capability review (Secure Exam Browser repository)
Evidence: Existing strengths include kiosk controls, face verification path, incident logging, and basic audio activity detection.
Gap in current implementation: Need stronger anti-spoof challenge-response, calibrated multimodal scoring, explainability, fairness testing, and policy-grade compliance controls.

CONSOLIDATED MAJOR GAPS
1) Lockdown-only security model
2) Weak anti-spoofing depth
3) False-positive burden
4) Fairness and accessibility risks
5) Privacy and compliance gaps
6) Weak multimodal fusion and confidence calibration
7) Limited explainability for incidents
8) Operational fragility under network and device issues
9) Limited GenAI-era integrity controls
10) Limited adversarial and red-team validation
